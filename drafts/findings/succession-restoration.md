# Succession & restoration: important findings

ACG began as an experiment in letting a cleared, burned and grazed landscape grow back into tropical dry forest. Forty years of research there, mostly in Santa Rosa, shows that forest structure, soils and many animals recover on their own once fire and grazing stop, while also showing that recovery is uneven and slowed by drought and vines.

## What we know

1. **Protection itself is the main restoration tool.** From the start, the plan was to buy land, stop fires and let the forest regrow on fire-scarred former ranchland ([Allen 2001](https://openalex.org/W1523436599)), with neighbours and schools as partners ([*Tropical Summer Clearance Sale* 1987](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0266467400002248); [Allen 1988](https://doi.org/10.2307/1310447); [Lanzen 2001](https://openalex.org/W2557183201); [Woodworth 2013](https://openalex.org/W2552248801)). Livestock were even put to work: horses spread seeds and kept invasive jaragua grass down in Santa Rosa ([Morera 2020](https://openalex.org/W3177322849)).

2. **Forests and soils rebuild over decades.** Studies use *chronosequences*, sites of known age since abandonment (10 to over 100 years), as a stand-in for watching one site over time. Soils along the Santa Rosa chronosequence are fertile enough not to limit tree diversity, and differences between soils help explain differences between sites ([Leiva 2008](https://doi.org/10.15517/rbt.v57i3.5494); [Leiva 2008b](https://doi.org/10.15517/rbt.v57i3.5495)). Soil carbon tends to rise as forest ages ([Powers 2015](https://doi.org/10.1079/9781780642109.0149)), and modelling suggests soil differences alone can produce large differences in forest biomass ([Medvigy 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.15848)). Tree growth, death and recruitment differ by stage ([Carvajal-Vanegas 2013](https://doi.org/10.18845/rfmk.v10i25.1371)) **[check: which stage grows fastest]**.

3. **Drought is the main hurdle for young trees.** Planted seedlings in old pasture died mostly during the dry season ([Gerhardt 1993](https://doi.org/10.2307/3235736)); other studies tested mowing, shade and root competition ([Gerhardt 1996](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0266467400009457); [Gerhardt 1996b](https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-1127(95)03700-4)).

4. **Animals come back with the trees.** Bird communities recover as forest matures ([Owen 2020](https://doi.org/10.5751/ace-01615-150125); see also [Hilje 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/f11060629)), leaf-litter frogs become more abundant ([Thompson 2023](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0266467423000305)), and howler monkey groups grew in regenerating forest ([Fedigan 1998](https://doi.org/10.1023/a:1020304304558); [Sorensen 2000](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0006-3207(99)00068-3)). Changing microclimate in altered forest also affects caterpillar performance ([Agosta 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2656.12647)).

5. **Lianas (woody vines) are increasing.** Over 11 years and more than 1,700 trees, fewer trees were liana-free, and lianas were linked to slower tree growth ([Becknell 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/ffgc.2022.838357); see also [Sánchez-Azofeifa 2017](https://doi.org/10.5194/bg-14-977-2017)).

6. **Recovery can be measured from the air and by sound.** Satellite, LiDAR (laser) and hyperspectral imagery can map forest age and successional stage ([Arroyo-Mora 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1744-7429.2005.00068.x); [Cao 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2015.08.009); [Zhao 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13193830); [Duan 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15020479)), and audio recordings can track biodiversity recovery ([Treuer 2014](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.4877807)). Cheap interventions can help: orange-peel waste spread on pasture in 1998 sped up regrowth ([Treuer 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/rec.12565)) **[check: size of effect]**.

## Long-term studies and data

The core sites are the Santa Rosa successional plots and chronosequence (10 to >100 years; [Leiva 2008](https://doi.org/10.15517/rbt.v57i3.5494); [Buzzard 2015](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2435.12579), 110 years), permanent plots measured for growth ([Carvajal-Vanegas 2015](https://openalex.org/W2682912311)) and lianas ([Becknell 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/ffgc.2022.838357)), repeat LiDAR flights 2005–2021 ([Liu 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15194677)), savanna maps 1985–2009 ([Vargas Ulate 2011](https://openalex.org/W2126020364)), wet-forest plots in Guanacaste National Park ([Calvo-Alvarado 2015](https://openalex.org/W1833749803)), and managed or planted stands at San Cristóbal and Horizontes ([Sierra-Parra 2022](https://doi.org/10.30878/ces.v29n2a9); [Villalobos-Barquero 2026](https://doi.org/10.18845/rfmk.v23i53.8795)).

## Open questions

- Will rising lianas slow carbon gain in young forest ([Becknell 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/ffgc.2022.838357))? How do drought years change it ([Calvo-Rodríguez 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/f12010106))?
- Why do sites of the same age differ so much: soil, history or seed sources ([Leiva 2008](https://doi.org/10.15517/rbt.v57i3.5494); [Medvigy 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.15848))?
- What happens in the transitions between stages ([Duan 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15020479))?
- Which active methods (planting, nucleation islands, waste) beat natural regrowth, and at what cost ([Treuer 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/rec.12565); [Sierra-Parra 2022](https://doi.org/10.30878/ces.v29n2a9))?

## Gaps

- **Data are rarely open.** 77 papers but 7 open datasets, and only 4 papers (all remote sensing, 2015–2017) link to one.
- **Plants dominate.** 59 papers are tagged plants; birds 4, mammals 4, insects 2, amphibians 1; none on fungi, soil life or reptiles.
- **One sector carries the theme.** Of 36 papers tagged to a sector, 27 are Santa Rosa; Guanacaste has 6 and Rincón de la Vieja, San Cristóbal and Horizontes 1 each. Wet and cloud forest succession is barely studied ([Calvo-Alvarado 2015](https://openalex.org/W1833749803)).
- **Few true time series.** Most studies compare sites of different ages at one moment; repeated measures of the same plots are few ([Becknell 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/ffgc.2022.838357); [Liu 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15194677)).
- **Output may be slowing.** 6 papers in 1986–1988, 10 in 1993–2000, 15 in 2001–2010, 36 in 2011–2020 and 10 since 2021 (a partial decade).
- **Grey literature.** 23 papers, many theses, have no DOI.
